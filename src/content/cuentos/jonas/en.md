The Bible tells of a man named Jonah who knew how to listen for God’s voice. One day, as he rested in the shade of his house, he heard God say, “Jonah, go to the great city of Nineveh. The people there are unkind and keep hurting one another. Tell them they need to change.”

Jonah frowned. Nineveh was very far away, it was enormous, and its people were known for being tough and quarrelsome. “Why does it have to be me?” he grumbled. “They can sort themselves out.” And instead of setting off toward Nineveh, he grabbed his bag and went the other way, all the way to the port of Joppa.

The port smelled of salt, fish, and wet rope. Seagulls squawked above the masts. Jonah found a ship sailing to Tarshish, on the far side of the sea, paid his fare, and climbed aboard. “No one will find me here,” he thought. He went down into the hold, lay down among the sacks and barrels, and fell fast asleep.

But God had not forgotten Jonah.

Out at sea, a mighty wind began to blow. The waves rose like mountains of water, and the ship went up and down, creaking and rocking from side to side. The frightened sailors each prayed in their own way and threw the cargo overboard to make the ship lighter. The captain went down into the hold and shook Jonah awake. “How can you sleep through this storm? Get up and ask your God for help!”

Jonah climbed up on deck, soaked by the rain. The sailors looked at him anxiously. “Who are you? Where are you from? What have you done?” Jonah hung his head. “This storm is my fault,” he admitted. “God asked me to do something, and I ran away. Put me into the water, and the sea will grow calm.”

The sailors didn’t want to. They rowed with all their might to reach the shore, but the wind blew harder and harder. At last, with heavy hearts, they asked God to forgive them and let Jonah down into the sea. At that very moment the wind dropped, and the water became as smooth as a mirror. The amazed sailors thanked God.

And God still had not forgotten Jonah.

Up from the deep swam an enormous fish, as big as a house. It opened its huge mouth, and Jonah, wrapped in a swirl of water, slid inside, safe and sound. It was dark and damp in there. It smelled of seaweed, and very close by he could hear the great fish’s heartbeat: thump, thump, thump.

Jonah spent three days and three nights inside the fish. He had plenty of time to think. And there in the dark, he prayed: “My God, I’m calling to you from way down here. I ran away from you, but you haven’t left me alone. If you give me another chance, I’ll go wherever you ask.”

On the third day, God spoke to the fish, and the fish swam to the coast. With a great splosh, it left Jonah on the sand of a beach. Jonah stood up with seaweed in his hair and sand in his ears, and he felt the sun on his face. The sky had never looked so beautiful.

Then he heard God’s voice again: “Jonah, go to Nineveh and take my message.” This time Jonah did not run away. He shook off the sand, fastened his sandals, and set off.

Nineveh was so big that it took three days to walk across it. It had towering walls, markets full of voices, and streets where children and donkeys scampered about. Jonah walked through them, calling out, “God has seen the wrong you are doing! You have forty days to change!”

And something happened that nobody expected: the people of Nineveh listened. They stopped fighting, they stopped cheating one another, and they said they were sorry. Even the king took off his royal robe, put on plain clothes, and sat on the ground like everyone else. When God saw that the city was truly changing, he forgave it.

But instead of being glad, Jonah was cross. “I knew it!” he said to God. “I knew you were kind and patient and that you would forgive them. That’s why I ran away!” He went out of the city, built himself a little shelter out of branches, and sat down to watch, very sulky indeed.

The sun was scorching. So God made a plant with broad green leaves grow up beside Jonah, giving him lovely cool shade. Jonah was delighted with his plant. But the next morning at dawn, a little worm nibbled it, and the plant withered away. The sun blazed again, a hot desert wind blew, and Jonah complained, “Oh, my poor plant! It was such a good plant!”

God spoke to him gently: “Jonah, you feel sorry for a plant you didn’t sow or water, that grew up in one night and withered in another. Shouldn’t I love Nineveh, a city full of families, little children, and even animals?”

Jonah said nothing and looked out at the city in the distance. One by one, the houses were lighting their lamps, like small stars. Perhaps that was when he understood that God had given him a second chance, and wanted to give one to everybody else as well.
