According to the Bible, in a village near the Sea of Galilee, there lived a boy who woke up every morning to the smell of fresh bread. His mother kneaded the dough very early, while the sky was still pearl grey and the fishing boats were slowly coming back to shore.

That day, the boy heard a buzz of footsteps and voices in the street. “Jesus is on the other side of the lake!” the neighbors were saying. “He heals people who are sick, and he tells stories that warm your heart.” Many of them shut their doors, tied on their sandals and set off.

“Mom, can I go too?” asked the boy, hopping up and down beside the table. His mother looked at him for a moment and smiled. Then she took a linen cloth and wrapped up five little barley loaves, still warm, and two small fish she had cooked the night before. “For when you get hungry,” she said. “Stay close to the neighbors. And remember: shared bread tastes better.”

The boy tucked the bundle into his cloth bag and ran outside. The path followed the edge of the lake. The water sparkled like a mirror full of tiny sparks, gulls squawked over the fishing nets, and the grass on the hills was green and soft, dotted with little flowers. More and more people joined them: grandparents with walking sticks, mothers carrying babies, shepherds with their dog, and children who raced ahead and then came running back.

At last they reached a gentle hillside where Jesus was talking to everyone. The boy wriggled between the grown-ups’ legs until he found a spot on a flat rock. From there he could see Jesus clearly. He spoke calmly, smiled at the people near him and laid his hands on those who were sick. The boy listened so closely that he forgot all about his bag.

The hours slipped by without anyone noticing. The sun sank toward the lake, and the sky turned orange, then pink. Then the boy began to hear other sounds around him: a baby crying, an old man sighing, some brothers and sisters grumbling quietly. “I’m hungry,” said one. “And I’m so tired,” said another. They were a long way from home, and there was no market anywhere nearby.

Close to the rock stood the disciples, the friends who went everywhere with Jesus. Jesus turned to one of them, a man named Philip, and asked him where they could buy bread so that all these people could eat.

Philip’s eyes grew wide as he looked out over the hillside, covered with people as far as he could see. “Teacher, even if we saved up many months’ wages, it wouldn’t be enough to give everyone a tiny piece,” he answered, scratching his head.

The boy heard every word. He put his hand on his bag and felt the lumpy shape of the loaves. There were only five, and the fish were small. Besides, his own tummy was rumbling too. “What good are my little loaves with so many people?” he thought. “They’ll laugh at me.”

But then he remembered his mother’s voice that morning as she wrapped the bread in the cloth: shared bread tastes better. The boy took a deep breath, climbed down from the rock and gently tugged the cloak of another disciple, a man named Andrew.

“I have this,” he said, opening his bag. Andrew looked at the five barley loaves and the two fish, and a tender smile crept across his face. He took the boy by the hand and led him to Jesus. “There’s a boy here with five loaves and two fish,” he explained. Then he added, a little embarrassed, “Though I don’t see how so little can help so many.”

The boy blushed right up to his ears. But Jesus crouched down to his height, looked him in the eyes and took the bundle in both hands, as if it were the greatest gift in the world.

Then Jesus asked his disciples to have everyone sit down on the grass. There was plenty of grass in that place, fresh and springy, and people settled into groups, like big families gathered around an invisible table. A curious hush fell over the hillside. Even the baby stopped crying.

Jesus took the loaves, looked up to heaven and gave thanks to God. Then he broke them and handed the pieces to the disciples to pass around. He did the same with the fish. The boy couldn’t take his eyes off Jesus’s hands. He broke and broke, and the bread never ran out. The disciples came and went with their arms full, and came back, and their arms were filled again.

The old man who had been sighing ate. The baby’s mother ate. The grumbling brothers and sisters ate, and so did the shepherds, and even the dog got a crumb. There were thousands of people there that evening, and every single one of them ate until they were full. The boy ate too. He bit into his barley bread and closed his eyes. It had never tasted so good.

When everyone had finished, Jesus told his disciples to gather up the leftover pieces so that nothing would be wasted. The disciples filled basket after basket. The boy counted them on his fingers, and he ran out of fingers: twelve baskets full of bread!

It was already dark when the boy walked home with the neighbors. The first stars shone on the water, and frogs sang among the reeds. His mother was waiting at the door with a lamp.

“Did you eat your lunch?” she asked, giving him a kiss. The boy grinned from ear to ear. “I shared it, Mom. With everyone.” And as he told her the whole story, his words tumbling over one another, his mother listened with shining eyes.

“And what did the bread taste like?” she asked at last. The boy thought for a moment. “Like shared bread,” he said. “It tasted better than ever.”
