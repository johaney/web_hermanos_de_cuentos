Little Red Riding Hood lived near a forest. One morning, she packed bread and fruit for her grandmother, who was resting at home. Her mother showed her the path and said, “Go straight there, and do not tell strangers your plans.”

Little Red Riding Hood walked among tall trees and tiny flowers. A wolf stepped onto the path and asked where she was going. Forgetting the advice, she told him about Grandma’s house. The wolf suggested picking flowers while he took a shortcut.

The wolf arrived first and knocked. When Grandma opened the door, she was startled and hid in a cupboard. The wolf put on her cap and climbed under the blankets to wait.

When Little Red Riding Hood arrived, something felt wrong. “Grandma, what big ears you have!” “All the better to hear you,” came a strange voice. The eyes and teeth did not look like Grandma’s either. She stepped back from the bed and called for help.

A forest ranger nearby heard her. She came in, helped Little Red Riding Hood outside, and sent the wolf away. Grandma came out of the cupboard, and the three shared the bread and fruit. On the way home, Little Red Riding Hood remembered why it was wise to follow the agreed path and ask a trusted adult for help when something felt wrong.
