Once upon a time there was a donkey named Bruno who had worked at a mill for many years. He had carried so many sacks of flour that his back was as white as sugar. But Bruno had grown old, he walked more slowly now, and the miller no longer wanted him for carrying. “You’re no use for this anymore,” he would say, and he left Bruno in the stable without so much as a glance.

One morning Bruno thought, “My legs may be tired, but my voice is still splendid.” And it was true: his bray was so loud it could be heard on the other side of the river. So he decided to go to the town of Bremen, where he had heard that musicians played in the square, and become a musician himself.

Along the road he found a dog sitting by a fence with her ears drooping. Her name was Lola. “What’s the matter?” Bruno asked. “My owner is a hunter, and now that I can’t run as fast as I used to, he says I’m no use to him,” she sighed. “Then come with me to Bremen,” said the donkey. “I’ll bray and you’ll bark. Each with our own voice, and better together!” Lola’s ears perked up, and off she went with him.

A little farther on, sitting on a stone wall, was a cat with a face like a rainy Monday. Her name was Mina. “I don’t catch mice the way I used to,” she told them, “and at home they’d rather have a young kitten. They won’t even let me near the fire anymore.” “You have a lovely meow at night,” said Lola. “Come to Bremen.” Mina stretched, licked a paw and jumped down from the wall.

As they passed a farm, they heard a rooster crowing with all his might from the top of a gate. His name was Rufo. “I’ve woken this farm every morning of my life,” he explained, “but now they’ve bought a clock with a bell, and they say I’m not needed.” “With a voice like that?” cried Bruno. “Come with us to Bremen. Each with our own voice, and better together!” So Rufo shook out his feathers and hopped down.

And so the four friends went on their way, chatting and practicing as they walked. The donkey kept the beat with his hooves, the dog barked, the cat meowed and the rooster crowed. It didn’t sound very pretty yet, but they were having a marvelous time.

Bremen was far away, and as evening fell they came to a thick forest. The sky turned purple and the stars began to come out. Bruno and Lola settled down under a big tree, Mina climbed onto a branch, and Rufo flew right to the top to keep watch.

From up there, the rooster spotted a tiny light in the distance. “Friends, there’s a house!” he called. “I bet they have a nice warm corner.” All four were tired and hungry, so they headed toward the light through the trees, stepping on dry leaves that went crunch, crunch.

It was a wooden house with one lit window. Bruno, the tallest, peeked in carefully. “What can you see?” the others asked. “I see a table covered with food: bread, cheese, sausages and baked apples. And all around it, some robbers eating supper and counting coins that aren’t theirs.”

The four friends put their heads together to work out how to get in. Then Lola had an idea. Bruno rested his front hooves on the windowsill, Lola climbed onto his back, Mina climbed on top of Lola, and Rufo perched on Mina’s head. They looked like a very strange tower.

“One, two, three…” And all at once they began their concert: the donkey brayed, the dog barked, the cat meowed and the rooster crowed. “Hee-haw! Woof! Meow! Cock-a-doodle-doo!” The window rattled, the candles flickered, and the robbers jumped so high they dropped their spoons. “A monster!” they shouted, and ran out the back door and deep into the forest.

The four friends went inside and ate until they were nicely full. Then they blew out the candles and each found a spot: Bruno lay down on the straw in the yard, Lola stretched out behind the door, Mina curled up by the glowing embers in the fireplace, and Rufo flew up onto a beam in the roof. And they fell asleep.

After midnight, one of the robbers tiptoed back to see whether the monster had gone. Everything was dark. As he came into the kitchen, he saw two bright little dots by the fireplace: Mina’s eyes, for she had woken up. He stepped back in fright and tripped over Lola, who barked. He hurried into the yard, and Bruno let out a tremendous bray. And from the roof, Rufo crowed, “Cock-a-doodle-doo!”

The robber ran without stopping all the way back to the others. “In that house there’s a shadow with shining eyes, a guard who growls, a giant who roars and someone on the roof who won’t stop shouting!” The robbers were so frightened that they went far, far away and never came back. Nobody got so much as a scratch, but they didn’t forget that fright for a very long time.

Bruno, Lola, Mina and Rufo liked the little house in the forest so much that they decided to stay. They never did reach Bremen, but they didn’t need to. Every evening, as the sun goes down, they sit on the porch and give their concert. It doesn’t sound perfect, but the birds come close to listen, and the four friends know that each with their own voice, they are better together.
