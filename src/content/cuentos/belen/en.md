According to the Bible, long ago, in a small town called Nazareth, there lived a young woman named Mary and a carpenter named Joseph. They were newly married, and they were expecting a very special baby. An angel had told Mary that this child would be the Son of God and that his name would be Jesus.

One day a messenger arrived in town with an order from the emperor in Rome: every family had to travel to the town of their ancestors to have their names written on a great list. It was a census, a way of counting all the people in the land. Joseph belonged to the family of King David, and David’s town was Bethlehem, a long way from Nazareth.

So Mary and Joseph packed a bundle with bread, cheese, dried figs and blankets, and set off. Mary, whose tummy was already very round, rode on a little grey donkey with long ears. Joseph walked beside her, holding the reins and singing softly to make the journey feel shorter.

The road was long and dusty. It climbed stony hills and wound down through valleys where goats were grazing. The days were hot; at night a chilly wind blew, and they had to shelter beside the rocks. “Is it much farther?” Mary sometimes asked, tired. “Not much now,” Joseph would answer, squeezing her hand lovingly.

At last, one evening, they saw the white houses of Bethlehem in the distance. But the town was crowded with people who had also come for the census. There were families everywhere, loaded camels, children running and dogs barking.

Joseph knocked on the door of the inn. “Do you have room for us?” he asked. “My wife is about to have her baby.” The innkeeper shook his head. “I’m so sorry, we’re completely full. I couldn’t fit in one more person.”

Joseph knocked on another door, and another, and every time he got the same answer. Then someone saw how tired Mary was and pointed them to a stable, a simple shelter where the animals slept. “At least you’ll be out of the cold in there,” they said.

The stable smelled of hay and wood. Inside there was an ox chewing slowly, some sheep curled up in a corner and a few doves cooing on the beams. The little grey donkey lay down beside them with a sigh. Joseph swept the floor, lit a small lamp and made a bed of clean straw.

And that night, in the stable in Bethlehem, Jesus was born. Mary wrapped him snugly in strips of cloth, held him close and kissed his forehead. They had no cradle, so Joseph filled the manger, the wooden box the animals ate from, with fresh straw, and they laid the baby there. The ox and the sheep watched quietly, and their breath made the air cozy and warm.

That same night, high in the sky, a new star was shining, bigger and brighter than all the others. It shone over Bethlehem, still and faithful, as if it wanted to tell the whole world, “Here. It’s here.”

In the fields nearby, some shepherds were spending the night outdoors, watching over their flocks. They were sitting around a campfire, wrapped in their cloaks, when suddenly a huge light lit up everything around them. An angel of the Lord appeared before them, and the shepherds were very frightened.

“Don’t be afraid,” the angel said in a gentle voice. “I’ve come to bring you news that will fill all the people with joy. Tonight, in Bethlehem, the Savior you have waited for so long has been born. This is how you will know him: he is a baby wrapped in strips of cloth and lying in a manger.”

At once the sky was filled with angels, so many angels, singing praises to God and wishing peace to all the people on earth. The sheep lifted their heads, the shepherds stood with their mouths open, and the whole night seemed to glow.

When the angels had gone, the shepherds looked at one another. “Let’s go to Bethlehem!” they said. “Let’s go and see what God has told us about!” And they ran across the fields, carrying the smallest lambs in their arms, beneath the light of the star.

They found the stable, pushed the door open very gently, and there were Mary, Joseph and the baby lying in the manger, just as the angel had said. The shepherds knelt down, full of wonder, and told everything they had seen and heard. Mary listened and kept every word in her heart, the way you keep a treasure. Then the shepherds went back to their flocks, singing and thanking God all along the way.

But the shepherds weren’t the only ones who had seen the star. Far away, in the lands of the East, some wise men who studied the sky every night had spotted it too. “It’s the sign that a king has been born,” they said. They loaded their camels with gifts and set off on a long journey, crossing deserts and mountains, never losing sight of the star.

Some time later, the star stopped over the house where the child was. The wise men were filled with joy. They went inside, saw Jesus in his mother’s arms and knelt before him. Then they opened their chests and offered him their gifts: gold that gleamed like the sun, frankincense that filled the air with its scent, and myrrh, a sweet-smelling and very precious resin.

Mary smiled and held her son close. Outside, high in the sky, the star went on shining over Bethlehem, still and faithful, like a promise of hope for the whole world.
