Mara planted a seed in a blue pot and watered it carefully. The next morning she ran to see it. The soil looked just the same.

“Why has it not come out?” she asked. Her grandfather explained that seeds work slowly underground: first they grow roots, then they reach for the sun.

Each day Mara watered it, gave it sunlight, and waited. One Tuesday a tiny shoot appeared, so tiny it almost looked like a green smile.

Some days Mara wanted to dig through the soil to see what was happening. Her grandfather suggested drawing the pot each afternoon. She noticed that, even when nothing seemed to happen above ground, the soil changed color and held the water.

After the first shoot came two leaves. Mara told her friend and gave her another seed to plant. They put their pots by the window and compared their drawings, without rushing.

Mara smiled too. She had learned that waiting did not mean doing nothing: it meant caring for something as it grew.
