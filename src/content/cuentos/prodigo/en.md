Jesus once told this story to the people who gathered to listen to him. There was a man who had two sons and a lovely farm, with silvery olive trees, grapevines climbing up the hills and a dirt road that led right to the front door. It always smelled of bread, grass and ripe figs there.

The older son was hardworking and serious. He got up with the sun and spent all day in the fields. The younger son, on the other hand, spent hours gazing at the horizon. “I bet life far away is much more fun,” he thought.

One day, the younger son planted himself in front of his father. “Father, give me now the share of your things that will be mine one day,” he said. “I want to go and live far away.” The father’s heart sank, but he did not shut the door on him. He divided up what he owned and gave his son his part.

A few days later, the younger son sold everything, filled a purse with coins and set off down the dirt road without looking back. His father stood at the door until he saw him disappear behind the last hill.

The son reached a faraway country, with noisy cities and markets full of shiny things. There he bought party clothes, threw feasts and surrounded himself with people who laughed along with him while he paid. He spent without counting, without thinking about tomorrow. Every day the purse was a little lighter, until one morning he opened it and there was not a single coin left.

Just then, a great drought came to that country. The harvests failed and food grew scarce. His party friends vanished one by one. Nobody gave him anything to eat.

The young man had to look for work, and the only job he could find was looking after a farmer’s pigs. He spent his days in the mud, his fine clothes in tatters, and he was so hungry that he looked enviously at the carob pods the pigs were eating. But nobody gave him even that.

One evening, sitting on a stone, he thought about home. He remembered the smell of bread, the long table and his father’s laugh. “At my father’s house, even the workers have more bread than they need,” he said to himself. “And here I am, so very hungry. I’ll go back. I’ll tell him I was wrong, wrong toward him and toward God. I’ll tell him I don’t deserve to be his son anymore, but ask him to let me work for him like one of the others.”

And he set off. It was a long journey, and he made it on foot, with dusty feet and an empty stomach. So he wouldn’t forget what he wanted to say, he practiced it with every step, very quietly: “Father, I was wrong. I don’t deserve to be your son anymore. Let me work for you.” He said it going up hills and coming down them, crossing rivers and falling asleep under the trees.

What the son didn’t know was that, ever since he had left, his father had gone out to the door every evening to watch the dirt road. Every evening, as the sun went down behind the hills, the father stood there for a while, shading his eyes with his hand, waiting.

And one evening, far in the distance, he saw a small figure, thin and tired. Even though it was a long way off, the father knew him at once. His heart filled with tenderness, he hitched up his robe and ran down the road, faster than he had run in years.

The son saw him coming and stood still, not knowing what to do. Before he could say a word, his father wrapped him in an enormous hug and kissed him again and again. In a broken voice, the young man began his speech: “Father, I was wrong, toward God and toward you. I don’t deserve to be your son anymore…”

But his father didn’t let him finish. He turned to the servants, who were running up behind him. “Quick! Bring the finest robe and put it on him. Bring a ring for his hand and sandals for his feet. And prepare a great feast, because today we celebrate! My son was lost, and now he’s found.”

That night, the house was filled with lights, music and dancing. It smelled of fresh bread and party food. The younger son, washed and wearing new clothes, kept looking at his father, as if he could hardly believe so much love.

Meanwhile, the older son was coming back from the fields, tired after a long day’s work. As he got close to the house, he heard the music and the clapping. He called one of the servants over and asked what was going on. “Your brother has come back,” the servant explained, “and your father has thrown a party because he’s home safe and sound.”

The older son grew very angry and refused to go in. He stayed outside with his arms folded, staring at the lights in the window.

So his father came out to find him too. He sat down beside him on the stone bench. “Father, I’ve worked for you for so many years,” the older son said, hurt. “I’ve always done what you asked, and you’ve never thrown a party for me and my friends. And now he comes back, after spending everything, and you celebrate!”

The father rested his hand gently on his son’s shoulder. “My dear son, you have been by my side all this time, and everything I have is yours too,” he said. “But we had to be glad. Your brother was lost, and now he’s found. Come in with me. The party isn’t complete without you.”

Jesus didn’t say what the older brother decided. But he did tell what that father was like: a father who waited at the door every evening, who ran to hug the son who came back, and who went out to look for the son who stayed outside. A father with open arms for both of his sons.
