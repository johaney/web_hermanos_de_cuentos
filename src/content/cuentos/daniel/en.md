The Bible tells of a man named Daniel who lived long, long ago in the great city of Babylon. He had come there from far away when he was young, and by now his hair was streaked with gray. He was so wise and so honest that King Darius had chosen him as one of his most important advisers.

Daniel worked in the palace, a vast place with stone columns, gardens full of fountains, and lions made of blue tiles on the walls. He helped the king make good decisions, he never kept anything that wasn’t his, and he always told the truth. The king trusted him so much that he was planning to put him in charge of the whole kingdom.

And Daniel had a habit he never forgot. Three times a day, in the morning, at noon, and in the evening, he went up to his room, opened the window that faced Jerusalem, the city where he had grown up, knelt down, and talked to God. He thanked him, told him his worries, and asked for his help. Three times a day, by the open window.

But in the palace there were other advisers who didn’t like how much the king cared for Daniel. “Why him and not us?” they muttered in the corridors. They looked for some mistake Daniel had made so they could accuse him, but they couldn’t find a single one. Daniel didn’t lie, didn’t cheat, and did his work well.

“There’s only one way to catch him,” said one of them with a crooked smile. “His God. Daniel will never stop praying to him.”

So they made their plan. They went to see King Darius, bowed low, and said in honeyed voices, “Long may you live, Your Majesty! We have a new law for you. For thirty days, no one may ask anything of any god or any person, only of you. And anyone who disobeys will be put into the lions’ den.” The king liked feeling so important, and he signed the law with his ring. In that kingdom, once the king had signed a law, no one could change it, not even the king himself.

Daniel heard about the new law. He stood thinking for a moment. Then he went up to his room, opened the window that faced Jerusalem, knelt down, and talked to God, just as he always did. Three times a day, by the open window.

The jealous advisers, who had been spying from the street, hurried to the palace. “Your Majesty, didn’t you sign a law? Well, Daniel isn’t obeying it. He still prays to his God three times a day.”

When King Darius heard this, he was filled with sadness. He realized he had been tricked. He was very fond of Daniel, and he spent the whole day, right up until sunset, trying to find a way to save him. But the advisers kept reminding him, “A king’s law cannot be changed.”

At nightfall, they took Daniel to the lions’ den, a great stone cave in the ground. The king went with him and said, his voice trembling, “Daniel, your God, whom you are always faithful to, will take care of you.” They lowered Daniel into the den, rolled a large stone over the entrance, and the king sealed it with his ring.

Inside it was dark and smelled of straw. Little by little, Daniel made out large shadows moving about: they were the lions, with their thick manes and golden eyes glowing in the dimness. Daniel took a deep breath and closed his eyes. “My God, I am in your hands,” he whispered.

Then a soft light filled the cave. It was an angel sent by God. The angel went up to the lions and gently closed their mouths. The lions blinked, yawned without a sound, and lay down on the floor, as calm as big cats by the fireside. One of them curled up close to Daniel, as warm as a blanket. And there, in the middle of the night, Daniel fell asleep.

Meanwhile, back at the palace, King Darius couldn’t sleep. He didn’t want any supper, he didn’t want any music, and he didn’t want anyone near him. He tossed and turned in his bed, thinking about his friend.

As soon as the sky began to grow light, the king leapt out of bed and ran to the den. With his heart in his mouth, he called out, “Daniel! Daniel! Has your God kept you safe?”

From the bottom of the cave came a calm voice: “Good morning, Your Majesty! My God sent his angel, and the lions have not harmed me at all. I have done nothing wrong, not against God and not against you.”

The king was overjoyed. He ordered the stone to be moved and Daniel to be lifted out of the den. Daniel came out safe and sound, without a single scratch, just a few bits of straw in his hair. Down below, the lions were still stretched out, looking as if they’d had a very good night’s sleep.

That same day, King Darius wrote a letter to all the peoples of his kingdom. In it he said that Daniel’s God took care of his people and protected them from every danger. And the king never again listened to those who wanted to do harm.

Daniel went on helping the king for many years, with the same wisdom and honesty as always. And every day, in the morning, at noon, and in the evening, he went up to his room, opened the window, and talked to God. Three times a day, by the open window.
