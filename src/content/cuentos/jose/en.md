The Bible tells of a shepherd named Jacob who lived long, long ago in the land of Canaan. He had twelve sons, and one of the youngest was called Joseph. Jacob loved him so much that one day he gave him a beautiful coat that reached down to his ankles and was full of colors: red like pomegranates, yellow like wheat, blue like the evening sky.

Joseph walked about in his coat, and the colors danced in the wind. But his older brothers watched with scowling faces. “Why does he get a coat like that and we don’t?” they muttered. And every day they felt a little more jealous.

What’s more, Joseph had very strange dreams, and he told everyone about them. “I dreamed we were tying up bundles of wheat in the field,” he said one morning. “My bundle stood up tall, and all of yours bowed down to it.” Another night he dreamed that the sun, the moon, and eleven stars bowed to him. His brothers grew even angrier. “So you think you’re more important than us?”

One day Jacob sent Joseph to find his brothers, who were looking after the flocks far from home. When they saw him coming, his colorful coat shining in the sun, they said to one another, “Here comes the dreamer.” Reuben, the eldest, begged them not to hurt him. But their jealousy weighed more than their love.

They took his coat away, and when a caravan of traders came by with camels loaded with spices, they sold Joseph for a few silver coins. The traders led him off on the road to Egypt. Joseph kept looking back at his brothers until he could no longer see them. And at home, his father Jacob wept for many days, believing he had lost his son forever.

Egypt was a huge country, with a wide green river, palm trees, and stone temples. Joseph had to work hard, far away from his family. He went through difficult times and was even put in prison when he had done nothing wrong. But he never stopped being honest or trusting God. And God was with him.

One night Pharaoh, the king of Egypt, had two dreams that kept him awake. He called for all his wise men, but none of them could explain them. Then someone remembered a young Hebrew man who understood dreams, and they went to fetch Joseph.

“I dreamed that seven fine, fat cows came up out of the river to graze,” Pharaoh said. “Then seven skinny cows came out, terribly skinny, and the fat cows vanished. Next I dreamed of seven ears of grain, plump and full, and behind them grew seven ears that were dry and empty. What does it all mean?”

Joseph listened carefully and replied, “Your Majesty, it is not I who know the answer, but God. Both dreams say the same thing. There will be seven years of wonderful harvests, with more wheat than anyone needs. After that will come seven years of hunger, when almost nothing will grow. You must store grain in the good years so that nobody goes hungry in the hard ones.”

Pharaoh thought this was so wise that he made Joseph governor of all Egypt. He gave him a ring, a gold necklace, and robes of fine linen. For seven years Joseph filled great storehouses with wheat. The granaries were so full that no one could even count the grain anymore.

Then the years of hunger arrived, just as Joseph had said. No rain fell, the fields dried up, and people from many lands traveled to Egypt to buy food. In Canaan too, in Jacob’s house, the bread was running out. “Go to Egypt,” Jacob told his sons. “They say there is grain there.”

So ten of the brothers set out on the journey. Only Benjamin, the youngest, stayed at home, because Jacob could not bear to part with him. When they arrived, they came before the governor of Egypt and bowed down to the ground to ask him for food. They didn’t recognize him: he was dressed like an Egyptian and spoke another language. But Joseph recognized them. And he remembered his dream about the bundles of wheat.

Joseph could have taken revenge. He had all the power of Egypt in his hands. But instead, he wanted to find out whether his brothers had changed. He asked them about their father and about Benjamin, and he heard them talking among themselves, full of regret: “All this is happening because of what we did to our brother Joseph.” Later, when they came back with Benjamin, he saw that they were ready to protect him no matter what.

Then Joseph couldn’t hold it in any longer. He asked his servants to leave the room, and he burst into tears. “It’s me, Joseph, your brother!” His brothers turned pale with fright. But Joseph hugged them one by one. “Don’t be afraid. I forgive you. What happened was very sad, but God has turned it into something good: because of it, today I can feed you and many other people too.”

The brothers hurried back to Canaan with carts full of food and some incredible news: “Joseph is alive, and he’s the governor of Egypt!” At first Jacob couldn’t believe it. But when he saw the carts his son had sent, his eyes filled with light.

Jacob traveled to Egypt with the whole family. When Joseph saw his father arriving, he ran to meet him, held him tight, and cried on his shoulder for a long time. He didn’t have his colorful coat anymore, but he didn’t mind. What he wanted most was this: to have his whole family together again.
