In a nest beside a pond, several ducklings hatched. The last egg took longer to open. Out came a big gray chick who looked different from the others. Some of the animals made unkind remarks that saddened him.

The little bird tried to swim and play with the others, but he was often alone. One day he explored beyond the pond. He met migrating birds, saw fields of flowers, and learned to find food for himself.

When winter came, the lake grew cold and he found shelter among the reeds. He remembered beautiful white birds he had seen flying in autumn. He hoped to meet them again, although he thought he could never look like them.

In spring, he returned to the water. His reflection surprised him: he had grown a long neck and white feathers. The white birds came closer. They were swans, just like him, and invited him to swim with them.

The young swan remembered the days when he had felt out of place. Those words had hurt, but they had never defined him. He looked across the pond, spread his wings, and followed his new friends, ready to discover the world in his own way.
